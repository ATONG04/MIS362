Learning Objectives

In this assignment, you will use Excel to examine blanks, invisible characters, ASCII codes, text, and numbers as forms of business data.

After completing this assignment, you will be able to:

  1. Distinguish truly blank cells from cells containing visible or invisible data.
  2. Apply Excel IS and length functions to test data content and type.
  3. Differentiate text used for meaning or identification from numbers used for arithmetic.
  4. Explain how ASCII characters support communication between people and machines.
  5. Evaluate how missing values, control characters, and incorrect data types affect business systems.

Assignment Overview

A cell that appears blank may contain a space, control character, or formula result. A person may see no content while a computer still detects data.

This assignment introduces blanks, data types, and ASCII as preparation for later work with databases, imports, validation, and data cleaning.

  • Human to human: language, labels, symbols, and context.
  • Human to machine: data, formulas, forms, commands, and clicks.
  • Machine to human: results, warnings, charts, prompts, and sounds.
  • Machine to machine: codes, delimiters, formats, and data types.

Text represents meaning, labels, codes, and identifiers. Numbers support arithmetic and quantitative analysis. Values such as ZIP codes, phone numbers, and product IDs may contain digits but should be stored as text when arithmetic is not meaningful.

Exercise 1 — Truly Blank or Not?
  1. Open the IsBlank worksheet.
  2. In B5, enter =ISBLANK(A5).
  3. Fill the formula down through B11.
  4. Compare cells that appear blank with cells containing zero, one, punctuation, one space, or multiple spaces.
  5. Use the results to determine which cells are truly empty.

Exercise 2 — Text, Numbers, and Data Types

Review the workbook examples that use functions such as ISTEXT(), ISNUMBER(), LEN(), and related checks.

  1. Open the worksheet containing the data-type examples.
  2. Complete the provided ISTEXT(), ISNUMBER(), and LEN() formulas.
  3. Compare the visible value with the function results.
  4. Identify at least one digit-only value that should be treated as text.

Consider why text and numbers serve different business purposes:

  • Text communicates words, labels, categories, codes, and identifiers.
  • Numbers support arithmetic, comparison, aggregation, forecasting, and analysis.
  • A value that contains only digits is not automatically numeric in business meaning.

Exercise 3 — Business-Relevant ASCII Characters

Open the ASCII and Codes worksheets. Focus on characters that commonly affect business data exchange:

  • Null: decimal 0
  • Tab: decimal 9
  • Line feed: decimal 10
  • Carriage return: decimal 13
  • Space: decimal 32

These characters may be invisible or appear only through spacing and line breaks, but software can still detect and process them.

Exercise 4 — The Absence of Data Is Information

Missing data can represent different business conditions. It may mean that:

  • the value is unknown;
  • the value does not apply;
  • the value has not yet been entered;
  • the event did not occur;
  • the system failed to capture the value;
  • the value was intentionally withheld.
