Learning Objectives

In this assignment, you will use Excel to transform raw purchasing data into useful information, compare alternatives, and support a SmartBean purchasing decision.

After completing this assignment, you will be able to:

  1. Differentiate raw data from information used to support a business decision.
  2. Standardize quantities expressed in different units of measure.
  3. Calculate and compare unit costs within appropriate product groups.
  4. Select and justify a reasonable Excel method for identifying the best value.
  5. Verify the results and develop an evidence-based purchasing recommendation.

Assignment Overview

SmartBean is comparing 30 purchasing options across several product groups, including coffee beans, tea, syrup, sugar, cups, lids, milk, muffins, napkins, cleaning supplies, and stirrers. Suppliers list quantities using pounds, ounces, grams, quarts, fluid ounces, liters, milliliters, and counts.

Package cost alone does not provide enough information to identify the best value. You must standardize comparable quantities, calculate unit cost, compare only like items, and explain the limitations of the result.

You will use these Excel features:

  1. Workbook and worksheet navigation.
  2. Ctrl + Home and Ctrl + End to determine a worksheet size and organization.
  3. Simple multiplication and division formulas.
  4. Relative cell references and Fill Down.
  5. Ctrl + ` to switch between values and formulas.
  6. Currency and decimal number formats.
  7. Grouping, sorting, filtering, conditional formatting, and ranking comparable purchase options.
  8. Explaining how spreadsheet output becomes information for a management decision.

Connection to Summative

This assignment develops spreadsheet and information-literacy skills so you better understand Summative01, which discusses how organizations combine people, processes, data, and technology to create useful information. This spreadsheet demonstrates that raw prices and package sizes do not become useful information until a person defines the decision, applies a process, verifies the calculations, and interprets the output.

Exercise 4 — Compare Like Items

The Group column identifies which purchasing options are reasonably comparable. For example, all coffee-bean options belong to the Coffee Beans group, while cups belong to the Cups group. Do not compare unit costs across unrelated groups.

You may explore value ranking in several reasonable ways:

  1. Sort or filter by Group and Unit Cost. Filter one group at a time, or sort first by Group and then by Unit Cost from smallest to largest. The lowest unit cost in each group is a possible best-value option.
  2. Use the Value Rank column. Enter ranks manually after sorting, or use a ranking formula that compares each row only with rows in the same Group. A rank of 1 should represent the lowest unit cost in the group.
  3. Use conditional formatting or a helper formula. Highlight the lowest Unit Cost within a group, or create a formula that labels a row Best Value when its Unit Cost is the group minimum.

These are examples, not the only acceptable methods. You may use another Excel approach that produces an accurate, understandable, and verifiable result.

  1. Compare only options assigned to the same Group.
  2. Use one reasonable method to identify the best value within each group.
  3. Check that your method does not rank unrelated products together.
  4. Review whether quality, storage, spoilage, vendor reliability, sustainability, or minimum order quantity could affect the final decision.
